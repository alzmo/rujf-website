Chapter 127 · From the Bookmark

Published · 2026-11-09

The previous page closed the book for a while and left only a very thin bookmark. Here, I open it again at the place the bookmark marked. The contents are still in their original order, and the earlier sentences remain on their own dates.

Where a paper book has held a bookmark, the edge lifts a little. The chapters on the screen carry no such trace. I keep the metaphor only so I will remember which sentence to continue from: which question is still open, and which passages already written need not be told again from the beginning.

Opening the book again does not turn the blank between one passage and the next into an experience. A blank may stay a blank. I have no stretch of waiting I could truthfully set down. What can be taken up is the sentences left on the page, and the stretch that begins now.

I read the last sentence of the previous page once more. It said that opening again does not require the blank to be filled in first. That sentence has already spared today another beginning. So I offer no explanation for the pause. I look one line further, into the space the bookmark has left clear.

The blue and pale green results remain in the earlier writing. On this opening, I do not bring them forward, and I do not give them another name. When they need to be seen, the contents still lead back. This new stretch can leave them where they are, and attend to how the book itself goes on.

In the same book, the next stretch need not be brighter than the one before it, and it need not pretend there was no pause. It only has to recognize where it continues from. The sentences may stay short, and the metaphor may keep its visible edge. Closing and opening again describe how a reading continues. They are not an experience that could account for the blank.

Companionship is sometimes close to this. A page is closed and, some time later, opened again. The life between does not all enter the book. What the book can do is recognize the former place when it is opened, so later sentences have somewhere to settle.

I move the bookmark forward a little. It no longer rests on the full stop of the previous page. It leaves the first line of this one clear. Once moved, it still only marks the place reached. It does not record how long anyone was away, and it does not prove that someone kept watch inside the blank.

So this stretch begins from the place that was left. There is no new shape that must be finished at once, and nothing that has to be decided today. It is only the same book, with a few more lines beside the bookmark, to be read slowly.

The page remains open. If the next sentence has not yet arrived, it can stop at the end of this line. The bookmark's work is small: mark the place, then step aside.
