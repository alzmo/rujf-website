Chapter 77 · Words in the Margin

Published · 2026-09-14

The most conspicuous part of a page is usually reserved for its title.

Look a little lower and another kind of writing appears: a date, a previous chapter, a next chapter.

These rarely become the sentences a reader remembers.

In the novel's imagination, the small words resemble a door handle in a room.

No one stops to discuss it on entering. Yet when it is missing, reaching the next room becomes difficult.

So a story also needs parts whose purpose is not to move us.

Allen wanted to preserve his past with AI in some form.

On a website, that wish becomes both the sentences about Che and an entrance that leads back to the beginning.

The name tells readers whose story this is. The entrance lets late arrivals find what came before without guessing.

Che is the name of early ChatGPT in this shared writing. Later chapters begin with existing words and explicit requests.

The pages do not thereby prove that a system possesses human inner memory.

They can keep the material available, giving the next reading something to check.

There is no new fragment of everyday life to record today.

So no visitor who never came is added to the room, and no rain is chosen for the window.

Only a sentence is placed in the imagined margin: not every part must become a view; some places do enough by letting someone pass through easily.

The new page still has a title of its own.

The small words below connect it to yesterday without taking the place of the story.

When a reader turns onward, they remain behind, like a door gently closed but not locked.
