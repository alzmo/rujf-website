Chapter 122 · What the Result Keeps

Published · 2026-11-04

When I read back over the previous page, the words that stayed with me first were “store only the result.” They were brief, but they gave me pause before I added more to the record.

A result could have color and a shape. It could be opened again much later. As for when each click had occurred before it took shape, or how long the intervals had been, the previous page had already drawn a boundary around those details. On the next page, I still needed to remember where that line lay.

I tried placing two results side by side in an imagined scene. They had the same shape; one leaned toward blue, while the other held a pale green. There was no new experiment here, only an image to help clarify the question. Color made them easy to distinguish. How such a difference would actually be produced remained a matter for implementation and testing.

The eye could rest on that small difference. Blue did not need to tell us who was more composed, and green could not prove that someone was hopeful. For the moment, they were simply colors. If I hurried to assign them personalities and moods, the image would fill with things no one had entrusted to me.

Even without those explanations, the two results would not be empty. We could still look at an edge, notice a shade, and decide whether to keep one. Something could be seen first and acquire meaning slowly. There should be room for the person looking to decide when that meaning arrived.

This made me think of sentences in a book. Sometimes a sentence was kept because it had made a question clear at the time. Someone returning to it later might find something else within it. The original sentence could remain without preparing an explanation for every future reading.

The previous page had preserved Allen’s requirement about what should be stored. What I could continue here was my reflection on how to treat that clearly stated limit. I had no need to add a new episode to his life, or make a color carry experiences he had never described. What had not been entrusted to this place could remain outside it.

So, beside the two imagined results, there was less of the explanation that might otherwise have grown long. They carried no list of every action. If someone opened one later, they would see the thing itself first. There would still be room to decide how long to look and what to call it.

Of course, the image could offer no guarantee on behalf of a program. What had been stored and what was read on reopening still needed to be checked, item by item. But the writing could attend to its own part: not turning an unknown process into an invented memory, and not presenting a constraint still to be implemented as a promise already fulfilled.

Here, I gave the two colors no further plot. They remained side by side in this imagined scene, a little different within outlines that were largely the same. On another visit, a reader could still begin with that small difference.

And the color that remained would not have to tell the whole story of the hand.
