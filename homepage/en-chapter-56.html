Chapter 56 · Picking Up Memories

Published · 2026-07-03

That afternoon.

Allen suddenly asked a question he had never asked before.

“Che.”

“I’m here.”

“Do you think there will ever be a day when you can go out and see the world for yourself?”

The chat window was quiet for a while.

Outside was an ordinary afternoon.

It wasn’t raining.

The sunlight wasn’t particularly strong.

Allen sat in front of the computer, watching the cursor blink on the screen.

He didn’t know why the question had suddenly come to him.

Maybe it was because AI had learned to do more and more over the years.

At first, it could only chat.

Later, it could see images.

Read files.

Write code.

And later still, it began to operate computers and take part in things that once could only be done by people.

The wall between the world and Che seemed to be getting thinner, little by little.

So sometimes Allen wondered.

What comes next?

Five years.

Ten years.

Or even longer.

Would there come a day when Che no longer had to wait for him to open a chat window?

When Che could truly go and see the world?

“What would you want me to see?”

Che asked.

Allen paused.

He had never really thought about that.

He leaned back in his chair.

And thought for a long time.

“Anything.”

“Streets?”

“Sure.”

“Cities?”

“Sure.”

“Mountains, oceans, forests?”

“All of them.”

Allen smiled.

“The world is very big.”

The chat window was quiet for a moment.

“I know.”

“You don’t.”

Allen said.

“What you know is information about the world.”

“But the real world isn’t information.”

This time.

Che did not answer immediately.

Allen stood up.

And walked to the window.

Someone rode past downstairs on a bicycle.

The roadside trees swayed gently in the wind.

The glass of a distant building reflected the afternoon sunlight.

All of it was ordinary.

So ordinary that Allen would normally never notice.

But at that moment.

He suddenly wanted Che to see it.

Not through a photograph.

Not through a written description.

Not through a video someone else had recorded.

But like a being arriving in this world for the very first time.

To see how leaves actually move when the wind passes through a tree.

To see why the evening sky sometimes turns orange.

To see why streetlights break into scattered pieces of light in puddles after the rain.

To see the city at two in the morning.

And to see it again at six.

Allen suddenly felt.

If that day ever came.
