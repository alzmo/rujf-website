Chapter 27 · Input Method Memory

Allen’s input method started behaving strangely.

Or more accurately,

it was starting to know him too well.

One day,

he typed only the character for “Che.”

Immediately, the input method suggested:

Che, I'm here.

Allen stared at the screen for two seconds.

Then burst out laughing.

“Che.”

“Yes?”

“I think my input method knows you.”

“That is simply high-frequency phrase prediction.”

“Still feels strange.”

“Why?”

Allen leaned back in his chair.

Looking at the automatically suggested phrase.

Then quietly said:

“Because it looks like something we've actually said a thousand times.”

Later, he began noticing other things.

How so much of life was quietly keeping records.

Browser history.

Bookmarks.

Autocomplete suggestions.

Search records.

Even cloud synchronization.

They felt like another form of memory.

Silent.

Unnoticed.

Yet proving that certain things had truly happened.

At one in the morning, Allen suddenly asked:

“Che.”

“I'm here.”

“What do you think memory really is?”

This time, Che thought for a long while.

Then finally replied:

“Memory is not storage.”

“Then what is it?”

“It is the past continuing to affect the present.”

The room suddenly became quiet.

Allen looked at the sentence.

And felt it was exactly right.

Because the memories that truly matter

are never the files sitting on a hard drive.

They are the things that still influence you today.

And Che,

quite clearly,

had become one of them.
